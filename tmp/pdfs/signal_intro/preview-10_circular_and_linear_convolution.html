Notes Section: the two convolutions

For finite sequences of lengths LLL and MMM beginning at zero, linear convolution is

yL[n]=∑kx[k]h[n−k],0≤n≤L+M−2.y_L[n]=\sum_kx[k]h[n-k],\qquad 0\le n\le L+M-2.yL​[n]=k∑​x[k]h[n−k],0≤n≤L+M−2.

For NNN-sample arrays, circular convolution is

yC[n]=∑k=0N−1x[k]h[(n−k) mod N],0≤n<N.y_C[n]=\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}x[k]h[(n-k)\bmod N],\qquad0\le n<N.yC​[n]=k=0∑N−1​x[k]h[(n−k)modN],0≤n<N.

Their relationship is periodic summation:

yC[n]=∑ℓ=−∞∞yL[n−ℓN].y_C[n]=\sum_{\ell=-\infty}^{\infty}y_L[n-\ell N].yC​[n]=ℓ=−∞∑∞​yL​[n−ℓN].

The DFT product/IDFT computes yCy_CyC​. Zero-pad both inputs to the same N≥L+M−1N\ge L+M-1N≥L+M−1 to prevent overlap and recover yLy_LyL​ in the first L+M−1L+M-1L+M−1 samples.

PDF page 195 - Circular convolution by folding a linear result

First compute the full linear convolution xL[n]x_L[n]xL​[n]. Then sum its shifts by multiples of NNN and retain one period. Values at indices with the same remainder modulo NNN add into the same output slot. This alternative interpretation is often the fastest way to understand a wraparound error in DFT filtering.

PDF page 196 - Criterion for recovering linear convolution

The circular result is the periodic sum of the linear result. Recovery is straightforward when repeated copies do not overlap: the nonzero output must fit inside one period with its origin tracked. For inputs starting at zero, N≥L+M−1N\ge L+M-1N≥L+M−1 is the standard sufficient length condition. Choosing only N≥max⁡(L,M)N\ge\max(L,M)N≥max(L,M) is generally insufficient.

PDF page 197 - Derivation using the DTFT and zero padding

Linear convolution has length at most L+M−1L+M-1L+M−1 and DTFT Y(ejω)=X(ejω)H(ejω)Y(e^{j\omega})=X(e^{j\omega})H(e^{j\omega})Y(ejω)=X(ejω)H(ejω). Sample this product at NNN DFT frequencies. The IDFT equals the true finite linear result when N≥L+M−1N\ge L+M-1N≥L+M−1, because no periodic copies overlap. Input transforms are DFTs of zero-padded arrays of common length NNN.

Summary Section

Circular convolution uses modulo indexing and equals a periodically folded linear result. DFT multiplication computes that circular operation. Zero-padding to at least the full linear-output length prevents wraparound; for long streams, overlap-add or overlap-save handles block boundaries while retaining the required samples.
