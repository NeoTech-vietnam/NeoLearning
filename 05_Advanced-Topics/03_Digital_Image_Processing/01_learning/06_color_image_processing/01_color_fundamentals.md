**Source:** Section 6.1, printed pp. 395–400 (PDF pp. 418–423).

### Cue Column

- How does human vision perceive color?
- What are hue, saturation, and brightness?
- Why is color representation tristimulus-based?

---

### Notes Section

Visible color depends on a light spectrum, object reflectance, and cone responses. Three cone classes motivate tristimulus representations. Chromatic light is described by radiance, luminance, and brightness; brightness is perceptual rather than a direct physical measurement.

Tristimulus values $X,Y,Z$ define chromaticity coordinates

$$x=\frac{X}{X+Y+Z},\quad y=\frac{Y}{X+Y+Z},\quad z=1-x-y.$$

Chromaticity separates color proportions from overall intensity. Hue identifies dominant color character; saturation measures dilution by white or neutral light.

---

### Summary Section

Color processing links spectral energy to three-channel visual responses, separating chromatic composition from intensity.
